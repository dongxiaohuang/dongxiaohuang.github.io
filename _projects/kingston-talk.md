A talk at Kingston University during Tencent’s campus visit on 7 October 2026: how data shapes game publishing decisions, and how AI makes that data easier to use.

**Publishing is a series of bets.** Signing, localising, launching and operating a game each commit time, money and people before the outcome is known. Each one needs data: similar games’ rankings and revenue, market size, acquisition cost, retention, review sentiment.

**The bottleneck is access.** That data is scattered across tools, defined differently by different teams, and slow to query when every follow-up waits on SQL.

**DataBrain makes the data askable.** DataBrain is the platform I lead for Tencent’s global publishing teams. Its AI agent reads a plain-English question, fetches the right sources (market data, Steam, reviews, creators, internal data), connects the signals, and answers with charts, sources and generated dashboards.

**Case study: PEAK.** How did a $7.99 co-op climbing game reach about 170,000 concurrent players? Four questions to the agent:

1. Concurrent players since launch showed four waves, not one spike: launch, and three later updates.
2. Steam reviews put playing with friends and value for money on top, with crashes on some PCs as the main risk.
3. Creator videos with millions of views clustered around each update, and the studio teased every update on TikTok days before.
4. One request built a dashboard of players, ratings and creator buzz that refreshes with the latest data.

The closing point: AI makes the data easier to use, and people still make the publishing decision.
